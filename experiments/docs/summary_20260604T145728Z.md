## Overview
This experiment evaluated the impact of various text preprocessing strategies (`prep_default`, `prep_minimal`, `prep_aggressive`, `prep_keep_digits`, `prep_emoji_tag`, `prep_segment`) on the `baseline` model to determine if specific normalization or tokenization techniques yield better results.

## Key Findings

1. **Performance Equivalence**: All 6 preprocessing strategies yielded **identical performance metrics** across all evaluation criteria (MAE, Accuracy, F1 scores) when using the `baseline` model.
2. **Execution Time Variation**: While predictive performance remained exactly the same, execution times varied based on the complexity of the preprocessing. 
    - `prep_emoji_tag` and `prep_keep_digits` were the fastest (~118-120 seconds).
    - `prep_segment` (which likely involves more complex word segmentation logic) was the slowest (~143 seconds).
3. **Model Insensitivity**: For the `baseline` model, altering the `XLMR_PREPROCESS_STRATEGY` configuration does not have any measurable impact on the downstream predictive performance. The baseline's feature extraction (TF-IDF) seems robust to or unaffected by these specific textual nuances.

## Conclusion & Recommendations

1. **Use Fastest Preprocessing for Baseline**: Since the metrics are identical, simpler strategies like `prep_emoji_tag` or `prep_keep_digits` can be favored for the `baseline` model simply due to their faster runtime.
2. **Further Testing on Neural Models**: While the `baseline` model is insensitive to these specific preprocessing variations, more complex models like `xlmr` might be much more sensitive. Future sweeps should test these preprocessing variants explicitly on transformer-based architectures where tokenization and text normalization can significantly impact embeddings.
